# Prompted recall

The owner remembers far more when shown a specific prompt than when asked an
open question. Every question to the owner follows this shape.

1. **Find the gap.** List what you need to know and why (for example, the job
   posting asks for X and the resume does not show it).
2. **Write concrete prompts.** One question per gap. Name the role, employer,
   year, system or artifact. Offer two to four specific options plus
   "Not sure". Yes/no is fine when the claim is binary.
   - Good: "At Epsilon (2021 to 2022), did your products use predictive ML
     models? Yes, I led them / Yes, my products used them / No / Not sure"
   - Bad: "What ML experience do you have?"
3. **Ask with the least typing.** Use tap cards, checkboxes or a numbered
   batch the owner can answer with numbers. Never require a typed paragraph.
4. **Act on the answer at once.** A yes goes into the record that needs it
   (resume, brand document, profile, application) in your best wording,
   marked "confirmed by owner, <date>". "No" removes the claim for good.
   "Not sure" keeps it out. Record the answer where future sessions read it,
   so the same question is never asked twice.
5. **Follow up only for one missing detail**, with options again.

## Before every job application

Extract the posting's requirements, match each against the resume and the
confirmed-facts record, and ask a prompted-recall question for every
requirement that is not clearly covered. Tailor the resume only after the
answers are in.
